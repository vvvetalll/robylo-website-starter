## 2. Why customers can’t find you

**Why nearby customers might not find you.**

It usually comes down to a few things.

- **You appear for your business name, but not for your services.** Your site or Business Profile may say too little about the work people actually search for.
- **Competitors show up in nearby towns you also serve.** Distance can affect local results, and competing profiles or pages may be a closer match for the search.
- **Your business information is inconsistent.** Old phone numbers, hours or business names across listings can make it harder for people to know which details to trust.
- **People visit your site but rarely get in touch.** On a phone, it can be hard to find the right service or take the next step if contact options aren’t clear.
- **You can’t tell which searches bring customers.** Search data and inquiries may be recorded separately, with no reliable way to match them.

## 3. Google Search & Maps

**Google Search and Maps work differently.**

A customer can see ads, local businesses and website pages in one search. Each appears for a different reason.

- **Sponsored results** Paid placements marked as ads.
- **Local / Maps results** Business profiles tied to a place or service area.
- **Organic website results** Web pages that match what someone is looking for.

- **Local results** Your Google Business Profile can show in Search and Maps. Relevance, distance and prominence all influence local results. An eligible business that visits customers can appear without showing its address.
- **Organic results** Individual pages can appear in Search. A useful service page explains the work, where it’s available and how to get in touch.
- **Paid advertising** Ads purchase a separate placement. Paying for an ad doesn’t improve organic rankings or Maps placement.

## 5. Chicago & the suburbs

**Serving several suburbs?**

You don’t need an office in every town.

- **Storefront** Customers visit a staffed business location during its stated hours.
- **Service-area business** You visit or deliver to customers and don’t serve them at your address. Keep the address hidden on your Google Business Profile.
- **Hybrid business** Customers can visit your eligible location and you also travel or deliver to them. The profile can show both the address and service area.

Use real service areas that fit how your business operates and follow Google’s Business Profile rules. Adding more areas doesn’t raise your ranking by itself.

A useful location page gives customers something specific, such as services available there, access requirements or how to book. Avoid virtual offices, invented locations and near-identical pages made just to rank.

## 7. Questions

Common questions

**Questions about local SEO.**

- **How long does local SEO take?** It depends on your starting point, the changes needed and the competition in the areas you serve. Google also needs time to process updates.
- **Can you guarantee first place on Google?** No. Local results vary with the search and the customer’s location. We improve the website and profile using the information we can verify.
- **Can my business appear on Maps without a storefront?** Yes, if it qualifies as a service-area business. Hide the address if you don’t serve customers there and list the areas you actually serve.
- **Do I need a new website for SEO?** Usually we start with the site you have. A rebuild makes sense when the existing site can’t support the fixes or pages you need.
- **Is SEO a one-time project or ongoing?** Either. An audit, profile setup or specific correction can be a one-time project. Ongoing work can include monitoring and improvements as needs change.
- **What’s the difference between SEO and Google Ads?** SEO improves the foundations for unpaid visibility. Google Ads buys separate placements and does not raise unpaid rankings.
- **What about AI search?** Clear content, accessible pages and accurate business information are useful foundations. No one can promise that your business will appear in an AI-generated answer.
- **Who owns my Google Business Profile and analytics accounts?** We recommend keeping ownership and administrative access in your business’s own accounts and granting collaborators only the access they need. We can discuss what that means for your setup.
