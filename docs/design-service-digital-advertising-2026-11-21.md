## 2. Where ad budgets leak.

Usually in the same few places.

- **Irrelevant searches** People looking for jobs, repair instructions or unrelated services can use up part of your budget. We review the searches that trigger your ads and use negative keywords to exclude patterns that don’t fit.
- **Wrong locations** Ads can reach people outside the area you serve, depending on campaign settings. We review location options and check what the campaign actually reaches.
- **Wrong landing pages** A homepage can make visitors search again for the service they wanted. A focused page answers that question and makes the next step easy to find.
- **Missing measurement** Without a way to measure calls or forms, it’s hard to tell which ads bring useful inquiries. We identify what can be tracked and where attribution is limited.

## 3. From search to call.

Every step after the click matters too.

- **The search** Choose searches that match your services and target the areas your business can serve.
- **The ad** Write a clear message that answers what the customer is looking for.
- **The page** Send the visitor to a relevant service page with a clear next step. A new landing page can be scoped separately as a Website Design project.
- **The inquiry** Measure calls, forms and other useful actions when suitable tracking is available. Some inquiries can’t be tied reliably to an ad.

## 4. Your money, in plain view.

Two costs, kept separate.

- **Ad spend** Money paid to the advertising platform. You can adjust the campaign’s average daily budget in Google Ads. Spend can vary by day under Google’s billing rules.
- **Service fees** Money paid for campaign setup, management and optimization. The service scope is discussed separately from your ad budget.

### What a performance review can cover

- Advertising spend
- Relevant inquiries where measurable
- Search terms and targeting
- Campaign adjustments
- Recommended next steps

Some calls and forms can be linked to an ad when tracking is available. Connecting those inquiries to customers or sales may require additional information from your business.

## 5. When Google Ads make sense.

And when to wait.

### Good fit

- Customers actively search for your services.
- You want visibility for specific services or locations.
- You have capacity to respond to inquiries.
- You have a realistic budget to test and improve campaigns.

### Better to wait

- Your business can’t respond to additional inquiries.
- Your website or landing page needs essential fixes.
- Your budget may be too small for a useful test.
- You haven’t decided which services or areas matter most.

Search ads can give you paid visibility while campaigns run. Organic SEO builds visibility separately. Paying for Google Ads doesn’t improve organic rankings.

## 6. Questions about Google Ads.

Eyebrow: Common questions

- **How much should I spend on Google Ads?** Start with the services and areas you want to test, what clicks might cost, and how many inquiries you can handle. A useful starting budget depends on those conditions.
- **Is Google Ads right for my business?** Search ads can be a good fit if customers already search for what you offer, you have a relevant page and you can respond to inquiries. Fix those gaps first if any apply.
- **How quickly can advertising start working?** Ads can begin appearing after setup and approval. When useful inquiries arrive depends on search demand, competition, your budget and the page visitors land on.
- **What is the difference between ad spend and management fees?** Ad spend is the money paid to the platform to run ads. Management fees cover agreed work on your campaigns, such as setup, monitoring and improvements.
- **Who should own the Google Ads account?** We recommend that your business keep administrative control of its Google Ads account and grant collaborators the access they need.
- **What’s the difference between SEO and Google Ads?** Google Ads buys ad placement while a campaign runs. SEO improves your website and business information for unpaid visibility. Paying for ads doesn’t raise organic rankings.
- **Do I need a new website or landing page?** You may be able to use an existing page if it matches the ad and clearly explains how to get in touch. If a new page is needed, it can be scoped as a separate Website Design project.
- **Can I adjust or pause my advertising budget?** You can change a campaign’s average daily budget or pause the campaign in Google Ads. Google’s billing rules still apply to spend already incurred. Any service terms are discussed separately.
