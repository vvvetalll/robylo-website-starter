# Homepage messaging and copy review

2026-11-18 · Claude (design and copy) · Status: **Proposed** — messaging research and copy recommendation only. No WordPress or Elementor change.

## Sources

- AGENTS.md, AI-CONTEXT.md, docs/ROBYLO-CONCEPT.md, decisions.md, Homepage V2 handoff and design log.
- **Current copy:** owner's full-page screenshot of the staged homepage (2026-11-18). The public robylo.com still shows Coming Soon ("Something Good Is in the Works." plus links to the five client sites), checked in a browser on 2026-11-18.
- **Proposed copy:** ChatGPT's "Digital Partner for Small Business" version, supplied by the owner.

Labels used below: **Fact** = verifiable in the repo, the screenshot or the live sites. **Judgement** = my professional opinion. **Hypothesis** = expected effect on conversion, not proven.

## 1. Bottom line

- **The current homepage copy is better than the proposed rewrite in most places.** It is more concrete, more natural and makes fewer claims. The proposal replaces specific lines with general ones and repeats the service list three times.
- **Neither version differentiates ROBYLO yet.** Both describe what hundreds of small agencies offer. The one real, ownable difference — *you work directly with the person who builds and runs your website, search, ads and tools* — is implied but never said plainly. ("One team" in the current copy actually works against it.)
- **The location is missing.** "Chicago" appears only in the footer. For a local studio selling to local owners, that is the cheapest credibility gain on the page.
- **Recommendation:** keep the current structure and most of its lines; make a small number of targeted changes: name the region, state the direct-contact difference once in the hero, remove the repeated service list, correct "One team", and make the Selected Work intro verifiable. Full copy in section 4.

## 2. Answers to the ten questions

**1. Does it differentiate ROBYLO?** No — neither version. *Judgement:* "direct communication, hands-on implementation, practical expertise, long-term involvement" are claims nearly every small agency makes, so stating them does not differentiate. What can differentiate ROBYLO: (a) the client talks to the person doing the work, with no account manager in between; (b) one person covers website, Google presence, ads and automation, so the pieces actually fit; (c) the work is local and live — five Chicago-area sites anyone can open today. Copy can point at these; proof (case study, named clients) does the rest.

**2. Will an owner understand it immediately?** Mostly yes for the current version. "Good business. Hard to find?" names a real feeling, and the sub-line says what you do. Missing: *where* (Chicago) and *why you rather than anyone else*. The proposal's hero sub-line ("the digital side", five capabilities in one sentence) is slower to read.

**3. Is "Digital Partner" useful?** *Judgement:* no, as a public label. It is one of the most common phrases in agency marketing, says nothing about what you do, and nobody searches for it. It is fine as an internal idea (long-term relationship), but the page should express it through specifics ("the same person handles updates, fixes and the next improvement"), not the label. For the page title and meta, use plain search language: *Websites & Local Search for Chicago Small Businesses*.

**4. Natural American English or AI-sounding?** The current copy reads mostly natural. The proposal has several typical AI tells:
- "Real websites for real businesses."
- "Launch isn't the finish line."
- "help you move forward", "the digital side", "take care of the work behind them", "built properly", "No unnecessary complexity"
- Stacked adjective lists: "clear, fast, easy to use, and ready to grow with you".

**5. Repetition across sections?** Yes, in the proposal. The same five-item list (websites, local visibility, advertising, automation, ongoing support) appears in the Hero, again in The Big Picture, and again as the four service rows. "We connect / work together / one partner" appears in Big Picture and What We Do. "Ongoing support / keep things running / stay involved" appears in Hero, Big Picture and How We Work. The current copy repeats the service list too (hero sub-line + diagram + rows), but less.

**6. Practical value without unsupported promises?** Current: mostly good. "Reports you can read" and "Nothing you have to babysit" are concrete and safe. The proposal adds soft promises: "measurable customer inquiries" (implies results), "ready to grow with you". The "Get found · Win customers · Save time" row exists in both; acceptable as aims, not claims.

**7. Too many capabilities?** Four services are fine for the structure (the diagram and service rows are built for four). The problem is **weight**: the portfolio proves websites, and partly local search. It does not show ads or automation work. *Recommendation:* lead the hero with being found (website + Google), keep ads and automation in the service rows, and do not add "ongoing support" as a fifth headline item (the proposal does).

**8. Fit with layout and typography?** Right-column paragraphs sit in a column about 384 px wide at 1440 (about 55 characters per line at 16 px). The current hero sub-line is 115 characters (3 lines in the screenshot). The proposal's is 158 characters (about 4 lines), heavier than the H1 beside it, and its Services intro is similar. Budget used for my version: right-column paragraphs ≤ 140 characters (≈3 lines at 1440, ≈4–5 lines at 390); two-tone H2 second lines ≤ 36 characters, so they stay within two lines at 30 px on mobile.

**9. Does the portfolio support the positioning?** Partly. *Fact:* five live, Chicago-area sites in five industries. That supports "we build good websites for local businesses" well. It does not yet support "partner", "long-term" or ads/automation. What would close the gap, only with verified data: one line per card saying what ROBYLO did (e.g. *Website, local SEO, ongoing support*) and how long (*Since 2024*, the AREA 17 pattern from the portfolio research), plus the planned Chicago Mounting case study.

**10. What in the current copy is better and should stay?**
- Hero headline and its echo in the contact section ("Hard to find?" → "Easy to find.").
- Services intro: "Each one works on its own. Together they cover how customers find you, what they see, and what happens after they get in touch."
- All four service descriptions, especially "make it easy to call, book or buy", "in Google Search and on Maps", "with reports you can read", "take follow-ups, scheduling and other routine work off your plate".
- All three How We Work headings and bodies, especially "Nothing you have to babysit."

What the proposal does better and I kept: "Tell us what you're working on" (easier for an owner who doesn't know what they need), and "One partner" correctly avoids implying a team.

## 6. Why this should convert better — hypotheses, not results

1. **Location raises relevance.** Owners of local service businesses tend to prefer a provider who knows their area. Naming Chicago in the first screen should increase "this is for me" recognition. *Hypothesis.*
2. **Direct contact is the real objection-killer for small owners.** Common complaints about agencies are hand-offs, slow replies and not knowing who does the work. Saying once, plainly, that you work with the builder addresses that before the visitor scrolls. *Hypothesis, needs owner confirmation that it is true (see open decisions).*
3. **Concrete beats abstract.** Lines that name a task the owner recognizes ("call, book or buy", "follow-ups, scheduling") are easier to believe than adjective lists. *Judgement, widely accepted in conversion copywriting, not measured here.*
4. **Less repetition, faster proof.** Removing the third repeat of the service list lets the reader reach Selected Work with less reading. *Hypothesis.*
5. **Lower-commitment contact.** "Even if it's a small one" should make it easier for owners with a small problem to write. Small jobs are often how retainers start. *Hypothesis.*

How to check with little traffic: a 5-second test with 5–10 real local owners (what does this company do, where, why contact them), and tracking contact-form starts after launch. A/B testing is not realistic at expected traffic.

## 7. Open decisions for the owner

1. **Confirm the direct-contact claim.** "You work directly with the person who builds it" and "The same person handles updates" must be true in practice. If work is sometimes handed to contractors, use "You talk directly to the person managing your project" instead.
2. Big Picture headline: new "Your website can't do it alone." or keep "A website is just the beginning."
3. Where do card links go — the live site or a project page? This sets "Visit site ↗" vs "View project".
4. Per-card "what we did · since" line: provide verified services and start year per client, or leave it out.
5. Approve the page title and meta description.
