## Part 1 — What the owner approved (recorded, not reinterpreted)

From the 2026-11-20 brief, section "Approved" list and sections 1–7:

- **Customer-first positioning:** ROBYLO takes the headache out of managing the digital side of a small business, so owners can focus on running it.
- **Reduced complexity:** owners should not need to learn SEO, WordPress, Google Ads, CRM, analytics or automation platforms. Explain the benefit before the technical term.
- **Practical digital partnership:** one point of contact for related digital work; handles both initial projects and ongoing management of the agreed scope.
- **Client ownership:** clients keep ownership of and access to their digital assets; ROBYLO can run everything operationally without locking the client in (also [decisions.md, 2026-11-19](decisions.md#2026-11-19--client-ownership-and-flexible-management)).
- **Clear expectations:** say what is included, what is not, and what results can reasonably be expected. Do not imply ROBYLO handles all IT, cybersecurity, hardware or business operations.
- **Page hierarchy:** every important page answers, in order: the problem → what ROBYLO does → why trust → what working together looks like → how to start.
- **Writing rules:** plain American English, short sentences, familiar situations, concrete examples, reassuring but professional; no jargon, buzzwords, slogans, exaggeration or invented claims.

Not approved: the exact phrases "You run your business. We handle the digital side." and the supporting line, and everything Claude proposes below.

## Part 2 — Independent assessment

### 1. Is the positioning clear and credible?

**Clear: yes. Credible: only if it is narrowed and proven.**

- It is the right problem for this audience. A contractor or a dental office manager does not want to "build a digital presence"; they want the phone to ring and someone to deal with the website. "We take care of it" speaks to that directly. The previous positioning ("Solve the right problem. Build it properly.", "Four areas of expertise") talked about ROBYLO's craft; this talks about the owner's relief. That is a real improvement.
- The credibility risk is scope. "We handle the digital side" is a big promise from a founder-led studio. It becomes credible when the page immediately says *which* digital things (four) and *how* they are handled (agreed scope, one contact, the client owns everything).

### 2. "You run your business. We handle the digital side."

| Test | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Natural | **Yes.** Reads like something a person would say. Clean parallel structure. |
| Memorable | **Moderately.** Easy to recall, but the pattern "You focus on X, we handle Y" is very common among agencies and managed-service firms, so it will not be remembered *as ROBYLO*. |
| Specific | **No.** "The digital side" can mean anything from a website to email, phones, computers and Wi-Fi. A visitor may assume ROBYLO is IT support. |

**Recommendation:** keep it as the headline. It passes the "understood in three seconds" test better than any line on the site today. Then make the second line do the specific work: name the four things, the area, the one point of contact and ownership. Do not add adjectives like "reliable" or "trusted" to the headline; they weaken it.

### 3. Does it differentiate ROBYLO?

**The concept alone does not.** "Done-for-you, one partner, focus on your business" is the standard pitch of many small agencies. What actually differentiates ROBYLO is narrower and checkable:

1. **One point of contact across four areas that usually come from different vendors** (web designer, SEO person, ads agency, someone for software).
2. **The client owns everything**, and ROBYLO says so in writing and helps with transitions. Many small-business owners have been burned by a vendor who owned their domain or Google account. This is the sharpest trust message available, and it is already approved.
3. **Plain scope and plain answers**: what's included, what isn't, what to expect.
4. **Local and real**: six live Chicago-area sites, founder named.

Put these four into the page as facts, not as adjectives. That is where the differentiation lives.

### 4. Does it create unrealistic expectations?

**Yes, in three places, unless addressed:**

| Risk | Where it comes from | Fix (proposed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "ROBYLO is my IT person" | "the digital side", "everyday business technology" (supporting line) | Never use "everyday business technology". Add one plain "What we don't handle" line on Services. |
| "They'll get me customers" | "customers find them", "more local customers" | Describe actions and outputs ("show up in Search and on Maps"), never outcomes ("more customers"). Keep the existing no-ranking-promise wording. |
| "They're on call for anything, anytime" | "handle", "take responsibility", "managed" | Always add "the agreed work" / "within the scope we agree". Do not state response times unless the owner can keep them for every client. |

### 5. How to present the four services

Lead with the owner's question, then one plain sentence of what ROBYLO does, then the service name as a label. The technical name stays in navigation, URLs, SEO fields and on the service page.

| Question (headline) | What we do (one sentence) | Label / link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Need a website that gets people to call? | We build or redesign your site so it's clear on a phone and easy to call, request a quote or book, then keep it updated for you. | Website Design & Development → |
| Want more people nearby to find you on Google? | We set up and look after your Google Business Profile and the pages Google needs, so you show up in Search and on Maps. | SEO & Local Visibility → |
| Paying for ads but not sure what you get? | We set up and run Google Ads for the jobs you want more of, and show you which calls and forms they brought in. | Digital Advertising → |
| Too much time on office busywork? | We set up simple tools that answer new inquiries, send reminders and move information where it needs to go. **[Confirm: automatic first reply is offered]** | AI & Business Automation → |

Notes on the brief's examples: "Need help reaching potential customers through advertising?" is the weakest; "reaching potential customers" is marketing language. The ads question works better when it names the owner's real doubt (paying without knowing the return).

### 6. What to simplify or remove

See Part 6 for line-level changes. In summary:

- **Homepage:** replace the hero idea with the approved concept; simplify "What we do" to the four question-led rows and drop the jargon sub-lists ("WordPress development", "Technical SEO", "Workflow automation"); keep Big Picture, Selected Work and How we work.
- **Services:** the H1 "Solve the right problem. Build it properly." describes how ROBYLO works, not what the owner gets. Lead with the owner's relief; move the craft line down to the standards section, where it belongs. Remove jargon from "Includes" lists. Add a short "What we don't handle" line.
- **Website Design:** the page is long (nine sections) and partly written for people who care about how websites are built. Keep the structure (composition is approved) but rewrite the technical bits from the owner's side: "Edited with Elementor" → "Easy to edit yourself"; "SEO basics: titles, descriptions, headings, sitemap" → "Set up so Google can read it". Lead the "Includes" section with what the owner does *not* have to do.

### 7. Trust without invented claims

Everything below is real or can be made real without inventing anything:

1. **Live work you can open.** The six sites, with "Visit site" links. Add the year the relationship started ("Since 2024") only where the owner confirms it.
2. **A named founder with a real photo** (About), and the same name in the contact section: "You'll hear back from Vitalii."
3. **Ownership in writing.** One sentence near every CTA: "Everything we build stays in your name."
4. **Scope you can read before you pay.** "You'll get a written scope with what's included and what isn't." Only if the owner always provides one. **[Confirm]**
5. **A sample of what a client receives**, e.g. one anonymized page of a real monthly update. **[Owner to provide; never mock one up as real]**
6. **Real client words, with permission.** Ask one or two current clients (e.g. Chicago Mounting) for one or two sentences and permission to publish with name and business. Until then, no testimonials section.
7. **A low-risk first step**: "Send us your website and we'll tell you what we'd fix first." This builds trust by doing a little work up front. **[Owner decides whether to offer it]**

## Part 3 — Brand positioning statement (proposed)

ROBYLO is a founder-led digital studio for small service businesses in Chicago and the suburbs: contractors, cleaning companies, dental practices and other local operators who don't want to become experts in websites, Google or software. We take care of the agreed digital work for them: building and maintaining the website, keeping the business visible on Google, running ads, and setting up simple tools that save office time. Owners deal with one point of contact, get plain answers about scope and cost, and keep ownership of everything we build.

## Part 5 — Voice and tone guidelines (proposed)

**Write like a competent local contractor explaining a job to a homeowner:** direct, calm, specific, no showing off.

| Rule | Before (current or brief) | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Benefit first, term second | "Technical SEO fixes" | "Fixing what stops Google from showing your pages" |
| 2. Say what we do, not what we are | "Four areas of expertise. One point of contact." | "Four things we can take off your plate. One person to call." |
| 3. No self-praise adjectives | "managed by one reliable partner" | "looked after by one point of contact" |
| 4. Describe actions, never outcomes | "more local customers find your business" | "show up when people nearby search on Google" |
| 5. Name the owner's tool, not ours | "Edited with Elementor." | "Easy to edit yourself." (mention Elementor in the sentence below) |
| 6. Explain the jargon once, or drop it | "Landing pages built for each campaign" | "A page built for each ad, so the click turns into a call" |
| 7. Concrete situation over abstraction | "make everyday operations easier" | "stop copying form entries into your calendar" |
| 8. Clear beats clever | "Solve the right problem. Build it properly." (as a page opener) | Keep it for the standards section; open with the owner's problem |
| 9. Always scope "handle" | "We handle everything." | "We handle the agreed work, from setup to monthly updates." |
| 10. One idea per sentence; under ~20 words | "Your website, search visibility, advertising, and business tools should work together. We connect them, keep things running, and help you move forward." | "Your website, Google listing and ads should work together. We set them up that way and keep them running." |

Also: contractions are fine ("we'll", "you're"); "you" more than "we"; no exclamation marks; avoid "solutions", "leverage", "empower", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "partner" as a self-description in headlines.

## Part 7 — Risks and recommendations

| # | Risk | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Digital side" read as general IT support | Specific supporting line + "What we don't handle" line on Services. Never use "everyday business technology". |
| 2 | Promise of results | Actions and outputs only. Keep "Nobody can promise rankings." No "more customers" claims. |
| 3 | A one-person studio promising to "handle" everything for many clients | Always "the agreed work"; no response-time promises until the owner can keep them; consider a stated limit on new clients only if true. |
| 4 | Generic slogan | Differentiate in the second line and on the page with the four checkable facts (one contact, ownership, plain scope, local live work). |
| 5 | Breaking approved copy pairs | Hero and final CTA on Home must change together or not at all. |
| 6 | Inconsistent voice across pages | Apply Part 5 to Home, Services and Web Design together, then to the remaining service pages as they are written. |
| 7 | Public pages currently show review markers | Remove or confirm before any public launch (still open from 2026-11-19). |
| 8 | "Partner" as a word | Use the idea, not the label. "Partner" in headlines reads as agency talk. |
